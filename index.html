Sensory Neuroscience · Nottingham Trent University

Understanding the sensory nervous system in health and disease

We study how the sensory nervous system, together with its surrounding microenvironment and vasculature, changes in conditions such as diabetes and after cancer treatment, and how these changes lead to long-lasting pain. Our work brings together sensory neuroscience, microenvironmental and vascular biology, with the aim of finding new ways to prevent and treat pain.

Research

We investigate how the sensory nervous system changes in disease to cause chronic pain, combining physiology, imaging and molecular approaches.

Sensory neuroscience and pain

How sensory neurons become sensitised and drive long-lasting pain, including pain arising from cancer treatment.

Neuroimmune interactions

How the immune system and the nervous system communicate, and how this contributes to neuropathy and pain.

Neurovascular biology

How blood vessels supporting the nervous system are altered in conditions such as diabetes, and the consequences for pain.

Imaging

Mapping the spinal cord vasculature

3D light-sheet reconstruction of blood vessels in optically cleared spinal cord tissue. Whole-tissue imaging allows us to study how the vascular network changes in disease. Scale bar: 500 µm.
